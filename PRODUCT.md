# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
Primary: U.S. men already on testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) who want to know whether they are overpaying and what switching clinics would cost. They arrive with a number (what they pay each month) and a state or ZIP code, often on a phone, often from a Reddit thread.

Close second: men about to start TRT, shopping online and in-person options and trying to see the real cost behind headline prices.

## Product Purpose
TRT Ledger is a free, independent price guide for TRT. It adds up the true first-year cost of online clinics (membership, intake, labs, medication, prepay terms), finds in-person clinics near any U.S. ZIP code, answers "Am I overpaying?" against clinics that serve the visitor's state, and lists direct-to-consumer lab prices. Success means a visitor leaves knowing what TRT really costs where they live, and trusts the number enough to act on it.

## Positioning
Every price links to its source and shows when it was last checked; prices are re-read from each clinic's own site (daily by the automatic check where a watch rule exists, at least weekly by hand otherwise) and stale prices are flagged automatically. Where a clinic doesn't publish a cost, the site says so and uses an assumption the visitor can see and change, rather than hiding the gap. Headline monthly prices are turned into first-year totals, which is the comparison clinics' own marketing avoids.

## Operating Context
- Visitors compare against what they already pay; the calculator and the ZIP search are the main entry points.
- Online clinic data: `data.json` (source of truth; `data.js` is generated), checked daily by `checker/` via GitHub Actions; jumps over 50% are held for the owner.
- In-person directory: federal NPI registry plus chain location lists, refreshed monthly by `pipeline/` into `clinics/` and `geo/`; state and city guides under `trt/` are generated by `pipeline/build_pages.py`.
- Price alerts go out through Buttondown (username `trtledger`).
- Hosted on GitHub Pages at trtledger.com (repo `trtledger/trt-ledger`).
- Launched and discussed on TRT subreddits; the owner is a TRT patient and discloses that they built it.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Not medical advice, not a pharmacy or clinic. No dosing advice, no claims about what a treatment does for health. Neutral definitions of terms only.
- Only clinics that publish prices are ranked; others go in a "doesn't publish prices" list.
- Clinic-provided estimates: when a clinic gives us its prices in writing but they aren't on its own website, we may show them, labelled "Clinic-provided estimate (month year)". We keep the written source and link it. They are listed after all clinics with published prices, in their own labelled group, and never get a "lowest" or "cheapest" label. They must be re-confirmed every 90 days (`reconfirmBy` in data.json); after that date the clinic goes back to the "doesn't publish prices" list automatically.
- A clinic that doesn't publish which states it serves is never presented as confirmed for a state; "lowest cost" status goes only to clinics confirmed for the visitor's state.
- Visitor inputs (ZIP, calculator numbers) stay in the browser; nothing is sent to a server except the voluntary alert signup.
- Business model: today no clinic pays to be listed or ranked and there are no affiliate links. This may change later, so the site states the current fact but does not promise "never".
- Every online clinic with prices has its own listing page under `online/<id>/` (generated by `pipeline/build_pages.py`): the first-year math line by line, contract terms, what's included, sources and check date, and a free-correction link. Home-page cards link to it, and `#clinic-<id>` on the home page opens that clinic's card.
- `/for-clinics/` states how listings work, that corrections are free for every clinic, and the fixed public prices of the three paid options (Claimed badge $49/month or $399/year, Competitor Price Watch $29/month, pricing report $149 or $449/year) with what each does and does not include. None changes order, math or labels. A clinic with a `claimed` entry in data.json shows a "Claimed by clinic · paid" tag; nothing else about its listing changes.
- Traffic numbers are shared with clinics on request, not published. Clicks on "Visit" links to clinic sites are counted per clinic with GoatCounter (cookieless, no personal data; `data-out` attribute). The counts are for our own use and for answering clinics that ask; no referral or click reporting is sold, and counts never affect order or labels.

## Brand Commitments
- Name: TRT Ledger. Voice: plain, direct, candid about uncertainty ("Check your state", "Unverified price", "Uses your assumptions").
- Kept separate from the owner's Locked Protocol app: the site does not mention or link to it, so the guide stays clearly independent.
- Visual direction (chosen 2026-11-04): the category standard, executed at full craft, not a themed or novelty look. Craft bar: Nuform, TRT Kingdom and Hims/Healthline for polish only; never their sales voice, health claims or imagery, since Nuform is a ranked clinic and the site must read as independent.
- Must avoid: clinical/medical, fintech/startup, and playful looks. Dark mode stays.

## Evidence on Hand
- Live price data for 18 online clinics (17 offering testosterone), in-person chains, and lab prices, each with source URL and checked date (`data.json`).
- About 12,600 in-person listings across 51 state pages and 566 city pages.
- No testimonials, press, user counts or partnerships exist; do not invent any.

## Product Principles
1. Show the math: every number is traceable to a source and a date, and assumptions are visible and editable.
2. Uncertainty is labelled, never hidden or rounded into confidence.
3. Answer the visitor's own question first: what they pay versus what is available in their state.
4. Independence over persuasion: no copy that reads like a clinic's sales pitch.
5. Fast and usable on a phone, since that is where most visitors arrive.
